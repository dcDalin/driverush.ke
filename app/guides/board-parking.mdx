Parking is one of the things candidates are tested on, and the Model Town Board has its own corner for it. The board has two parking zones, angle parking and flush parking, each with its own rules, and a rule about when you may drive through the parking area at all.

## Angle parking

Angle parking is also called ample or controlled parking. The bays are set at a slant to the aisle, at 45 degrees.

- **Who may use it:** strictly small cars, saloons. Buses and heavy vehicles do not park here.
- **Park from the farthest end.** Take the farthest free bay.
- **Park by forward gear.** Drive straight into the bay, nose first.
- **Leave by reverse.** Reverse out, giving way to anything in the aisle, then drive off forwards.
- **The way in and out:** the zone has a set entrance and exit, from both sides. On our board its aisle is two-way, with a way in and out at each end and a give way line at each, because you are joining another road when you leave. Keep left in the aisle and drive slowly: a car may pull out of a bay on either side.

The slant tells you which way to come from. A bay leans away from you in the direction you are driving, so you can turn into it without swinging wide.

## Flush parking

Flush parking is also called parallel or uncontrolled parking, and you will hear it called flash parking. It is parking in line with the kerb. The method is the other way round from angle parking.

- **Where it is:** on the left side of the road, in the direction of traffic.
- **Why it is uncontrolled:** it has no security.
- **Who may use it:** all vehicles except tractors and trailers.
- **It has an entry, but leave space for the exit.**
- **Park from the farthest end.** Take the farthest free box.
- **Park by reverse.** Drive just past the box, stop, and reverse into it.
- **Leave by driving forward.**

## Buses and heavy vehicles

Buses and heavy commercial vehicles are not allowed in the angle bays. They have their own spaces, and the method is the flush one: drive past the space and reverse into it, never nose first. Our board has no bus bay, so ask your school to show you theirs.

## Keep out of it unless you have to

Two of the handbook's three route rules are about parking:

1. Use the shortest correct route **without parking.**
2. Use the longest correct route if needed, for example when a continuous line blocks the short way, **still without parking.**
3. Parking is **only a last option.**

So the parking area is not a shortcut. If one route cuts through it and a longer route goes round, take the longer one.

## No parking here

You lose marks for stopping, parking or waiting in any of these places. A brief pause is not a stop, but standing still is.

- **On the roundabout.** Parking is one of the five things you must not do on it.
- **Beside a yellow kerb.** The yellow kerb means no overtaking, no parking, no waiting and no stopping.
- **On a double yellow line,** like the one down the middle of the three-lane road.
- **At a junction, or within 10 metres of one.**
- **On a pedestrian crossing.**
- **On a pavement, a footpath or the green.**
- **Near the brow of a hill, in a bus stop or in a clearway.** These are road rules an examiner may ask about. Our board has none of them.
- **Where a sign forbids it.** Learn <Link to="/road-signs/parking-prohibited">Parking prohibited</Link> and the other parking signs on the <Link to="/road-signs/pelican">Pelican chart</Link>.

## What to ask your driving school

- Where the entrance and the far end of the parking zone are on their board.
- Where buses and heavy vehicles park on their board.
- Whether flush parking is tested at your centre.

## In the trainer

<Link to="/model-town-board">Our trainer</Link> teaches both kinds the handbook's way. In an angle bay you drive in forwards and reverse out. In flush parking you drive past the bay, reverse in, and drive out forwards.

- **Watch it first.** <Link to="/model-town-board#unit-parking">The parking levels</Link> start with the rules, then a car does each move a step at a time.
- **Find the farthest free bay.** The parked cars stand in different bays from one attempt to the next, so the bay to take changes. It is the one with the green box.
- **Then drive it.** There are four parking tasks. They have a reverse gear: stop, pull the joystick back and hold it, and the back of the car goes where you point. With lane assist on, the car is steered into or out of the bay as you reverse.

You pass when the car is stopped inside the lines and straight. Driving forwards into or out of a bay, lane assist lets go, so the car goes where you point; reversing, parking assist helps the back of the car along the line. On the way, a brief pause is fine, but standing still for more than a few seconds on a pedestrian crossing, on the roundabout or beside a yellow kerb costs a star. Stopping in a bay, or at a stop or give way line, never does. In a task that is not about parking, nobody stops you for driving through the parking zone, but the drive is then not a pass, because parking is only a last option.
