The roundabout is where most mistakes on the Model Town Board are made. It is a meeting point of traffic, where more than two roads meet at a point. Its job is to let vehicles move in different directions without any obstruction or collision. These are its rules.

## Which lane to be in

Choose your lane before the continuous lines, because you may not change lanes once you reach them, or on the roundabout. Each lane has its own options, counted in the handbook's two codes: **2124** on the four-lane road and **215** on the three-lane road. In short:

- **Lane 1:** go straight (0°) or turn left (90°). Maintain lane 1 to exit.
- **Lane 2:** go straight (0°). Maintain lane 2 to exit.
- **Lane 3 of the four-lane road:** go straight (0°) or turn right (90°). Maintain lane 3 to exit.
- **Lane 4 of the four-lane road:** turn right (90°), U-turn (180°), Q-turn (270°), or go straight (360°) after a full loop. Maintain lane 4 to exit.
- **Lane 3 of the three-lane road:** go straight (0°) or turn right (90°) into lane 3, maintaining lane 3. Or shift to lane 4 to turn right (90°) into lane 4, make the U-turn (180°) or make the Q-turn (270°).

## On the roundabout

You are required to keep left and move in a clockwise direction on the roundabout. Give way to traffic already on it, which comes from your right. Its lanes are counted from the outermost lane to the innermost: lane 1 is the outer lane and lane 4 is the one beside the island. Each lane of the road goes round in the roundabout lane with its own number, with one exception: lane 3 of the three-lane road keeps to the roundabout's third lane to go straight or turn right into lane 3, and shifts to its fourth lane for its other three options.

The rules of the roundabout are five things you must not do on it:

- no stopping
- no changing lanes
- no parking
- no overtaking
- no waiting

A brief pause is not a stop. In the trainer, lifting your thumb off the joystick for a moment costs nothing, but standing still on the roundabout for more than a few seconds costs a star.

## The three parts of the roundabout

The handbook divides the roundabout into three parts:

- **The traffic island,** the green part in the middle, which controls how vehicles move round it.
- **The innermost lane,** lane 4. It is the only lane that allows a driver to go round and make a full circle (360°) from the four-lane road, and the only lane where a driver may make a 270° turn from the three-lane road. The Q-turn (270°) and going straight (360°) both go once round the island first, then out at their exit the second time they reach it.
- **The space,** lanes 3, 2 and 1. Drivers in these lanes may not make a full circle or a 360° turn: they take their exit the first time they reach it.

## Leaving the roundabout

Maintain your lane to exit. There is one rule to add, the **minus 1 rule**: from lane 3 or 4 of the four-lane (major) road onto the three-lane (minor) road, the exit lane is the entry lane minus 1.

- **Lane 1 turning left** keeps lane 1.
- **Lane 3 turning right** exits into lane 2, the middle lane.
- **Lane 4 turning right, or making the Q-turn,** exits into lane 3.
- **From the three-lane road onto the four-lane road,** you leave in the lane you went round in: lane 3 or lane 4.
- **Lane 3 of the three-lane road** shifts to lane 4 for a U-turn and comes back into lane 3, the innermost lane it has.

On our board a pale yellow cross runs through the roundabout, carrying the middle of each road in to the island. Going round, you pass a road's exit lanes and then its line, so leave before you reach the line of the road you want. You drive over the cross; it is not a line you must keep off.

Signal left as you go, and change lanes only further on, where the lines are broken.

## The common mistakes

The handbook names four:

1. Approaching in the wrong lane.
2. Leaving in the wrong lane.
3. Changing lanes on the roundabout.
4. Misreading the traffic lights, where a roundabout has them. Our board has none.

## What to ask your driving school

- **Who gives way.** We teach giving way to traffic already on the roundabout, which comes from your right. The handbook's Unit 3 does not say it, so ask your instructor how your test centre wants it done.
- **Their own board.** Boards are not all painted the same, so have your school walk you round theirs.

## Practise it

The roundabout unit of the <Link to="/model-town-board">Model Town Board simulator</Link>
teaches the roundabout rules, shows each turn driven a step at a time, and then
gives you seven roundabout tasks to drive yourself, from the dual carriageway
and the three-lane road. It stops you when you take a turn your lane does not
allow, change lanes on the roundabout or leave in the wrong lane, and it tells
you which lane to use. Standing still on the roundabout for more than a few
seconds costs a star. Lane assist holds your lane through a shaky thumb, but
the joystick decides: push steadily somewhere else and the car goes there. It
is free, with no signup.

To check what you know first, try the <Link to="/model-town-board/questions#q-roundabout">roundabout questions and answers</Link>.
