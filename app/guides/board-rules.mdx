## The three route rules

The examiner names a place to go. There is usually more than one way to get there, and the handbook gives three rules for choosing:

1. **Use the shortest correct route without parking.**
2. **Use the longest correct route if needed,** for example when a continuous line blocks the short way from your lane. Still no parking.
3. **Parking is only a last option.**

"Correct" is the word that matters. A short route that breaks a rule, such as crossing a continuous line or going the wrong way down a one-way road, is not a correct route at all.

Rule 2 is the one learners miss. Say you are in lane 2, the lines beside you are already continuous, and you are asked to turn left. Lane 2 only goes straight and you may not change lanes, so the short way is blocked. You go straight, turn back, and come round to the road you were asked for. That is the longest correct route.

You can try all three rules on the <Link to="/model-town-board">Model Town Board simulator</Link>, where the routes unit gives you a place to reach and stops you if you break a rule on the way. If you break no rule you can drive on wherever you like, but only the route you were asked for is a pass.

## Keep left, and know your lane number

You drive on the left in Kenya: keep left unless you are overtaking. Lanes are counted from the outermost lane, by the kerb, to the innermost, so lane 1 is beside the kerb and the highest number is beside the middle of the road.

## The roads on the board

- **The one-way traffic road.** Each side of the dual carriageway is a one-way traffic road: all vehicles on it move in one direction, with white continuous or broken lines dividing it into equal lanes. The central reserve separates the two sides.
- **The three-lane road.** Three lanes each way, with a double yellow line down the middle dividing it into two equal parts. Never cross a double yellow line.
- **The two-way traffic road.** One lane each way, where vehicles move in opposite directions, with a single yellow line at the centre dividing it into two equal parts.
- **The street.** A short two-way road joining the dual carriageway to the two-way road, beside the parking zone. Cars use it in both directions.

## Continuous lines and broken lines

- **A white dotted or broken line** between lanes means you may change lanes or overtake if the road is clear and safe. Check your mirrors and signal for 3 seconds before you move.
- **A white continuous line** means no changing lanes and no overtaking. On the board the lines turn continuous close to the roundabout, so choose your lane before you reach them.
- **A continuous yellow line** down the middle of a two-way road means stick to your side: no overtaking.
- **A broken yellow line** down the middle means you may overtake, or turn across it, if the road is clear.
- **A double yellow line** down the middle of the three-lane road means you never cross it, to turn or to overtake.

## The roundabout

A roundabout is a meeting point of traffic where more than two roads meet, there so vehicles can go different ways without obstruction or collision. Keep left and go round clockwise, giving way to traffic already on it, which comes from your right.

- **The five rules:** no stopping, no changing lanes, no parking, no overtaking and no waiting on the roundabout.
- **Your lane:** choose it before the continuous lines, then maintain it all the way round to your exit. Each lane has its own options, taught as two codes: 2124 for the four-lane road and 215 for the three-lane road.
- **The minus 1 rule:** from lane 3 or 4 of the four-lane road onto the three-lane road, you exit one lane further out (exit lane = entry lane minus 1).
- **All the way round:** only the innermost lane, lane 4, may make a full circle. Its Q-turn (270°) and its straight on (360°) both go once round the island before they leave.

## Kerbs and the central reserve

The yellow kerb is a yellow marking that protects the land, or field: the green part on the left side of the road. Treat it as the edge of the road and keep the car off it. A yellow kerb means no overtaking, no parking, no waiting and no stopping beside it. In the trainer a brief pause is fine, but standing still beside a yellow kerb, on the roundabout or on a crossing for more than a few seconds costs a star.

The central reserve separates the one-way traffic roads of the dual carriageway. A gap in it is an exit from the main road, or a U-turn onto the other side. Be in the lane beside the reserve first, and give way to traffic on the side you are joining.

## Stop, give way and crossings

- **<Link to="/road-signs/stop">Stop</Link>:** a red eight-sided sign (an octagon) with white letters, where you join a two-way traffic road. You stop every time, even with nothing coming. Look right, left and right again, and go only when the road is clear.
- **<Link to="/road-signs/give-way">Give way</Link>:** a red triangle with its point facing down and a white border. Slow down, or stop if necessary, and go only when it is safe. Traffic on the road you are joining goes first.
- **<Link to="/road-signs/pedestrian-crossing">Pedestrian crossing</Link>:** a safe, marked area where pedestrians have right of way. Slow down, and stop when pedestrians are crossing so they can get from one side to the other. Never stop, park or wait on the crossing itself.

Obey every sign on your route, in order. You can learn all {SIGN_COUNT} of them on the <Link to="/road-signs/pelican">Pelican chart</Link> or with <Link to="/road-signs/3d">3D signs</Link>.

## Parking

The board has two parking zones, and the route rules tell you to keep out of them unless there is no other way.

- **Angle (ample, or controlled) parking:** strictly for small cars, saloons. Park from the farthest end, drive in by forward gear, and leave by reverse. On our board its aisle is two-way, with a way in and out at each end.
- **Flush (parallel, or uncontrolled) parking:** on the left side of the road in the direction of traffic. It has no security, so it is uncontrolled. All vehicles may use it except tractors and trailers. It has an entry, but leave space for the exit. Park from the farthest end by reverse, and leave by driving forward.

<Link to="/model-town-board/parking">Parking on the Model Town Board</Link> covers both.

## What to ask your driving school

Boards are not all the same, and the handbook does not cover everything. Ask your instructor:

- to show you the board you will be tested on, and practise on it with you
- which lane the middle lane of the three-lane road may turn from at a junction
- whether your test centre still follows the 2016 handbook
