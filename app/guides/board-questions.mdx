## How to pass the Model Town Board test

NTSA does not publish the test's questions or how it is marked, so no one can promise you a pass. What the handbook does give is the rules every answer rests on. Learners who know these cold do well:

1. **Know your lane numbers.** Lanes are counted from the outermost lane, by the kerb, to the innermost. Say the number of the lane you are in before you move the car.
2. **Choose your lane before the continuous lines.** Where the white lines are broken you may change lanes or overtake; once they are continuous you may do neither.
3. **Know what each lane may do at the roundabout.** The option codes 2124 and 215 tell you, and the arrows painted in each lane show it. Maintain your lane to exit, and off the four-lane road onto the three-lane road use the minus 1 rule.
4. **Keep left, go round clockwise and give way to your right.** No stopping, no changing lanes, no parking, no overtaking and no waiting on the roundabout.
5. **Pick the route by the three rules.** Use the shortest correct route without parking, the longest correct route if needed, and parking only as a last option.
6. **Obey every line and sign on the way.** Never cross a double yellow line. Stop at a Stop sign, give way at a Give way sign, and at a crossing let pedestrians cross: they have right of way.
7. **Practise on the board you will be tested on,** with your driving school.

## Now drive it

Answering a question is one thing. Putting the car in the right lane is another. <Link to="/model-town-board">The Model Town Board simulator</Link> teaches each rule level by level, then gives you a task, lets you drive it, and tells you which rule you broke. It is free, with no signup.

Your driving school is still the place to practise on a real board, because the test uses a real one.
