Kenya does not have one driving licence. It has classes, and the class you apply for decides what you may legally drive. They are set out in the Third Schedule of the Traffic (Driving Schools, Driving Instructors and Driving Licences) Rules, 2020. DriveRush is not affiliated with NTSA, so confirm the class with your driving school when you enrol.

**The short answer for a car:** B1 is automatic only. B2 lets you drive both manual and automatic cars, and covers everything B1 does. B3 is the professional class for taxis.

## Every class, in the 2020 rules

| Class | What it lets you drive | Minimum age and requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AM, moped | A motorcycle up to 50 cc, with no passenger and no load | 16 |
| A1, light motorcycle | A motorcycle over 50 cc, with a passenger and up to 60 kg of load | 18 |
| A2, motorcycle taxi, couriers | A motorcycle over 50 cc, with a passenger and up to 100 kg of load | 18 |
| A3, three-wheeler | A three-wheeled motorcycle (tuk-tuk), up to 3 passengers and 100 kg | 18 |
| **B1, light vehicle automatic** | A car or light vehicle up to 3,500 kg with an **automatic** gearbox, up to 7 passengers, with one light trailer up to 750 kg. Not a manual car. | 18 |
| **B2, light vehicle** | The same vehicles with a **manual or automatic** gearbox, including vans under 3,500 kg not used to carry passengers. Also covers B1. | 18 |
| B3, professional | A taxi (a light vehicle up to 3,500 kg), manual or automatic, up to 7 passengers | 21 |
| C1, light truck | A truck over 3,500 kg and up to 7,500 kg | 22, and a B2 licence for at least two years |
| C, medium truck | A truck over 7,500 kg, with one light trailer | 24, and two years on C1 |
| CE, heavy truck with trailer | A truck over 7,500 kg with a heavy trailer or semitrailer, and articulated vehicles | 28, and four years on C |
| CD, hazardous materials | A heavy truck carrying hazardous materials | 30, and two years on CE |
| D1, van | A van with up to 14 passengers | 22 |
| D2, minibus | A bus with 14 to 32 passengers | 25, and three years on D1 |
| D3, large bus | A bus with 33 or more passengers | 30, and three years on D2 |
| D4, articulated bus | An articulated bus | Three years on D3 |
| E, special professional | Commercial driving in the C and D classes, licensed as EC1, EC, ECE, ED, ED1, ED2 and ECD3 | 21, and three months' practice in that class |
| F, drivers with a disability | The vehicle the driver was tested in | 18, with a medical certificate, assessment report and disability certificate |
| G, plant and equipment | Forklifts, graders, excavators, rollers and other plant | Endorsed on the driving licence |

The truck, bus and professional classes (C, D and E) also have a maximum age of 65 in the rules.

## What this means for most learners

**Learning in an automatic?** You can take B1, but you then may not drive a manual car. **Learning in a manual?** B2 covers both. If you might ever drive a manual, B2 is the safer choice.

**Schools label their courses differently.** Some list "B1/B2" as one course, and a few swap the labels on their price lists. Ask which car you'll learn in and which class the school will apply for, and check it against the table above. See [what driving schools in Kenya charge](/driving-schools-kenya) for each class.

**Riding a boda boda** for hire is A2. A tuk-tuk is A3.

## How to pick

Three honest questions, in order.

**1. What will I drive most often this year?** If the answer is "a saloon I share with my family", that points to B2, or B1 if it's an automatic and always will be. If it's a work van or pickup, ask your school which class covers it before you assume.

**2. Will my employer require a different class?** If your job involves driving a heavier vehicle, ask your employer which class they need before you enrol.

**3. Am I planning to do passenger-service work?** If yes, expect extra requirements on top of a basic licence. Ask NTSA or your school what they are. Start with the class you need now.

## The mistake most people make

Over-applying. Someone wants to be ready for "anything" and picks a heavier class "just in case", then trains on a vehicle they won't drive again. Apply for the class you'll actually drive.

## Then what?

Your class is chosen at the PDL stage, after you enrol at a licensed driving school. NTSA says the learner is required to enrol into one. See [choosing a driving school in Kenya](/driving-schools-kenya) and the [NTSA test guide](/ntsa-test).

Whatever class you're chasing, the test covers the Model Town Board, so [practise the Model Town Board](/model-town-board) before your lessons on it. Road signs and the Highway Code matter too. If you haven't drilled signs yet, [the Pelican chart](/road-signs/pelican) is the fastest entry point (free, no signup), and [3D signs](/road-signs/3d) teach you to read them from any angle.

For the full step-by-step process (licensed school, PDL, driver test, interim licence, Smart DL), the [2026 NTSA walkthrough](/blogs/ntsa-driving-licence-kenya-2026) is the companion piece to this one. And once you have the licence, the bit nobody studies for is in our [unwritten rules of Kenyan roads](/blogs/boda-bodas-matatus-unwritten-rules) post.
